	\item (\textit{AMC 8 1988}) A fifth number, $n$, is added to the set $\{ 3,6,9,10 \}$ to make the mean of the set of five numbers equal to its median.  The number of possible values of $n$ is


 $\text{(A)}\ 1 \qquad \text{(B)}\ 2 \qquad \text{(C)}\ 3 \qquad \text{(D)}\ 4 \qquad \text{(E)}\ \text{more than }4$


 
 \textbf{Answer: }C



\textbf{Solution}

The mean of the set $\{3, 6, 9, 10, n\}$ is $\frac{3+6+9+10+n}{5} = \frac{28+n}{5}$.

 If $n < 6$, the median is $6$.  Solving $\frac{28+n}{5} = 6$ yields $n = 2$, which is valid since $2 < 6$.

 If $6 \leq n < 9$, the median is $n$.  Solving $\frac{28+n}{5} = n$ yields $n = 7$, which is valid since $6 \leq 7 < 9$.

 If $n \geq 9$, the median is $9$.  Solving $\frac{28+n}{5} = 9$ yields $n = 17$, which is valid since $17 \geq 9$.

 Therefore, there are $\boxed{\textbf{(C)}\ 3}$ possible values for $n$.

	\item (\textit{AMC 8 1988}) Maria buys computer disks at a price of $4$ for $<dollar>5$ and sells them at a price of $3$ for $<dollar>$$5$.  How many computer disks must she sell in order to make a profit of $<dollar>100$?


 $\text{(A)}\ 100 \qquad \text{(B)}\ 120 \qquad \text{(C)}\ 200 \qquad \text{(D)}\ 240 \qquad \text{(E)}\ 1200$

 To profit $\$100$, she must sell $\frac{100}{5}=20$ batches of $12$ disks, for a total of $\boxed{\textbf{(C)}\  240}$ disks.

	\item (\textit{AMC 8 1988}) A \textbf{palindrome} is a whole number that reads the same forwards and backwards.  If one neglects the colon, certain times displayed on a digital watch are palindromes.  Three examples are: $\boxed{1:01}$, $\boxed{4:44}$, and $\boxed{12:21}$.  How many times during a $12$-hour period will be palindromes?


 $\text{(A)}\ 57 \qquad \text{(B)}\ 60 \qquad \text{(C)}\ 63 \qquad \text{(D)}\ 90 \qquad \text{(E)}\ 93$


 
 \textbf{Answer: }A



\textbf{Solution}

Consider two cases:

 Case 1: the hour is $9$ or less.  In this case, the time has $3$ digits, so it is in the form $A:BA$.  Since $A$ can be any digit from $1$ to $9$ while $B$ can be any digit from $0$ to $5$, there are $9\cdot 6 = 54$ three-digit palindromes.

 Case 2: the hour is $10$ or more.  In this case, the time has $4$ digits, so it is in the form $AB:BA$.  Since $AB$ can be $10$, $11$, or $12$, there are $3$ four-digit palindromes.

 Therefore, there are $54 + 3 = \boxed{\textbf{(A)}\ 57}$ total palindromes.
