	\item (\textit{AMC 8 1991}) An auditorium with $20$ rows of seats has $10$ seats in the first row.  Each successive row has one more seat than the previous row.  If students taking an exam are permitted to sit in any row, but not next to another student in that row, then the maximum number of students that can be seated for an exam is


 $\text{(A)}\ 150 \qquad \text{(B)}\ 180 \qquad \text{(C)}\ 200 \qquad \text{(D)}\ 400 \qquad \text{(E)}\ 460$

 \textbf{Answer: }C



\textbf{Solution}

We first note that if a row has $n$ seats, then the maximum number of students that can be seated in that row is $\left\lceil \frac{n}{2} \right\rceil$, where $\lceil x \rceil$ is the smallest integer greater than or equal to $x$.  If a row has $2k$ seats, clearly we can only fit $k$ students in that row.  If a row has $2k+1$ seats, we can fit $k+1$ students by putting students at the ends and then alternating between skipping a seat and putting a student in.

 For each row with $10+k$ seats, there is a corresponding row with $29-k$ seats.  The sum of the maximum number of students for these rows is 
 \[\left\lceil \frac{10+k}{2}\right\rceil +\left\lceil \frac{29-k}{2} \right\rceil = 20.\]There are $20/2=10$ pairs of rows, so the maximum number of students for the exam is $20\times 10=200\rightarrow \boxed{\text{C}}$.

	\item (\textit{AMC 8 1991}) For every $3^\circ$ rise in temperature, the volume of a certain gas expands by $4$ cubic centimeters.  If the volume of the gas is $24$ cubic centimeters when the temperature is $32^\circ$, what was the volume of the gas in cubic centimeters when the temperature was $20^\circ$?


 $\text{(A)}\ 8 \qquad \text{(B)}\ 12 \qquad \text{(C)}\ 15 \qquad \text{(D)}\ 16 \qquad \text{(E)}\ 40$

 \textbf{Answer: }A



\textbf{Solution 1}

The temperature increased by $12^{\circ}$, so it increased by $3^{\circ}$ four times.  Since each $3^{\circ}$ increase caused the volume of the gas to expand by $4$ cubic centimeters, the gas expanded by a total of $4 \cdot 4 = 16$ cubic centimeters.  Therefore, the original volume of the gas was $24 - 16 = \boxed{\textbf{(A)}\ 8}$ cubic centimeters.

 



\textbf{Solution 2}

Let $x$ be the temperature in degrees and $y$ be the volume of the gas.  Since a $3^{\circ}$ increase in the temperature causes a $4$ cubic centimeter expansion, the relationship between $x$ and $y$ is linear and has slope $\frac{4}{3}$.  Using point-slope form with the point $(32, 24)$, the equation of this relationship is $y = \frac{4}{3}(x-32) + 24$.  Substituting $x = 20$ gives $y = \boxed{\textbf{(A)}\ 8}$.

 


	\item (\textit{AMC 8 1991}) Each spinner is divided into $3$ equal parts.  The results obtained from spinning the two spinners are multiplied.  What is the probability that this product is an even number?


 
                    \begin{figure}[H]
                        \centering
                        \includegraphics[width=0.5\textwidth]{Problem/AMC_8_AJHSME_1991_22_Problem_Figure_1.png}
                    \end{figure}
                    
$\text{(A)}\ \frac{1}{3} \qquad \text{(B)}\ \frac{1}{2} \qquad \text{(C)}\ \frac{2}{3} \qquad \text{(D)}\ \frac{7}{9} \qquad \text{(E)}\ 1$

 \textbf{Answer: }D



\textbf{Solution 1 (Complementary Counting)}

The probability that the product is even can be found by subtracting the probability that the product is odd from $1$.  This approach is known as complementary counting.

 The product of two numbers is odd if and only if both numbers are odd.  The probability the left spinner lands on an odd number is $\frac{2}{3}$, while the probability the right spinner lands on an odd number is $\frac{1}{3}$.  So, the probability that the product is odd is $\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{2}{3} = \frac{2}{9}$, while the probability that the product is even is $1 -\frac{2}{9} = \boxed{\textbf{(D)}\ \frac{7}{9}}$.

 



\textbf{Solution 2 (Inclusion-Exclusion)}

The product of two numbers is even if and only if at least one number is even.  The probability the left spinner lands on an even number is $\frac{1}{3}$. The probability that the right spinner lands on an even number is $\frac{2}{3}$.  The probability that both spinners land on even numbers is $\frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{2}{3} = \frac{2}{9}$.  By the principle of inclusion-exclusion, the probability that the product is even is $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{2}{3} - \frac{2}{9} = \boxed{\textbf{(D)}\ \frac{7}{9}}$.
